# Improve from evidence

## Find the maintained source

Use the user's path or the skill identified in the conversation. Read its
entry, relevant references, callers, and repository instructions. Prefer the
maintained source over an installed copy. Leave managed installations intact;
if necessary, fork into a repository or writable skill home and state which
copy later work must use. Resolve ambiguity when it changes the target.

## Choose a consequential change

Compare the user's objective with observed work and the skill's instructions.
Look for causes of repeated corrections, unsupported completion claims,
unavailable commands, and instructions that obstruct useful judgment.
Local checks passing does not establish that the method catches every
required property. A concrete incident can justify a scoped repair; retain
uncertainty about broader conclusions.

Choose the change within current authority, including simplification or
justified retention. Do not force a fixed number of changes, a new checker,
or a review sequence. Preserve real dependencies and explicit requirements.
If removing a step also removes its evidence, establish how the required
outcome will still be verified. Propose work beyond authority with a concrete
next step.

When replacing a workflow, extract its outcomes, gates, approvals, and budgets.
Preserve how check inputs are created and updated at completion so later
checks retain their meaning. Do not copy the state graph or import past
counters as fresh authority.
Inspect project helpers by responsibility, even under a former workflow path.
Keep useful evidence or queue helpers until a verified replacement supports
their removal. Report runtime dependencies that still prevent standalone use;
do not invent queue, ownership, or identity infrastructure to replace them.

## Preserve improvement responsibility

For project-local skills, inspect what feedback changes on the next attempt.
The revised skill must require assessment when feedback exposes a weakness,
and at completion. Repeated review corrections may justify critical review
of the complete change, a better check, or a design change. Let the working
agent choose from the evidence and retain useful methods with a reason.

Require the agent to apply chosen repairs to the current work, finish the
requested result, and verify affected conclusions. Preserve required outcomes
when changing checks. Preserve what requires review, including new, untracked
deliverables within that scope. Self-review does not replace required independent
review; artifact metadata does not establish reviewer independence. Use
revision-bound evidence when required, and report unmet requirements honestly.

Keep useful lessons in the skill, checks, or project documents. Use remaining
task budget, honor approvals and explicit stops, and avoid recursive improvement
work. New evidence can justify reassessment. Brief evidence, a decision, and
verification in the existing work record or handoff suffice; do not require
dedicated state or disposition labels.

## Verify the revision

Preserve exact command meaning and provenance. Resolve changed paths and
dependencies; run appropriate checks after inspecting their side effects.
New gates require working implementations and verification. An unavailable
checker leaves a visible gap, not permission to drop the outcome.
For migrated checks, exercise affected input creation and completion updates;
one successful invocation can miss these paths.
Do not replay destructive or external actions merely to test instructions.

Check the revised guidance against the observation that motivated it and a
case where the existing method should remain. Report inspection and execution
separately. A successful wording check does not prove improved work outcomes;
use authorized trials and later work to assess that result.
